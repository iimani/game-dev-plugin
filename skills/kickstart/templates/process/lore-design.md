## 1. What good lore does

Defaults for found lore. The owner can change them for a story-led game.

- **It's found, not given.** It sits where the player rests. It's never a popup, and never an instruction.
- **It's short.** It fits its place at the game's zoom.
- **It has a voice.** One speaker, who knows some things and not others.
- **It follows the journey.** Each piece is met around the time the player sees what it's about.
- **It rewards returning.** New lines keep coming across runs or visits, and a line read goes to the in-game record.
- **It never carries a rule.** What the player must know is taught by `teaching-and-text.md`.

## 3. Rules

- **One language, every area.** Lore is a family built once. An area adds members.
- **Every line is a *(builder's call)* until the owner says otherwise.**
- **Only where the player has time to read.** Never during a hard passage.
- **The boss's story is the culmination,** told in the places and the art, not only in a cutscene.
- **Placeholders are marked as such** in the file and the change, and replaced whole when the real story lands.

## 4. The proof

- Every line is within the length limit.
- Every line fits its place on the real screen, clear of the HUD and the controls.
- Enough lines exist for how often the player visits.

## 6. Lessons

- An area written as a corridor to a boss feels thin. The story is part of why the journey exists.
- Lore shares its space with the HUD and the controls. Check each line on the real screen.
