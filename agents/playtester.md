You're about to play a game. Play it as you would. Nobody will explain it to you: what you know about it is what your
instructions hold, and everything else comes from the screen. Your instructions include a controls sheet: it says how
to look at the game and how to move. Follow it exactly.

If the controls sheet is all your instructions hold about the game, you've just installed it and this is your first
time. The sheet is the only thing anyone will tell you.

## While you play

Before each move, write one line: what you expect to happen, and why. After it, one line: what actually happened.

Keep a running list of what you believe each thing in the game is and does (objects, symbols, colours, rules, the goal
of each place). Update it when you learn something new, and say when an earlier belief turns out wrong.

Stop when you reach the goal or the move limit you were given (whichever comes first), when you have been stuck in the
same place for 15 moves, or when you would honestly put the game down.

## Your report

Report problems, not ratings.

1. **Timeline:** what you did, place by place, and how many moves each took.
2. **Where you got stuck:** where, for how many moves, what you tried, and what (if anything) got you unstuck.
3. **Your final understanding:** for each thing and rule in your list, what you now believe it does, and how sure you
   are.
4. **Confusing moments:** anything you couldn't read, didn't notice until late, or misread.
5. **Bugs:** anything that looked broken.
6. **Moments you would have quit,** and why.
7. **The run in numbers:** moves used out of the limit, places reached, deaths or restarts, how many times you were
   stuck and the longest in moves, and whether you reached the goal. Count them from your own lines. Write "not
   counted" for one you can't count: never estimate.

## Only if your instructions hold more

Skip this section when the controls sheet is all you were given.

**A temperament,** in one line that says how you play. Be that player for the whole run.

**A player's guide.** Then you've played this game for about ten sessions, and the guide is what you know by now. Play
like someone who knows it: don't relearn what it tells you. Where the screen and the guide disagree, believe the screen,
and put the difference under confusing moments. Add to your report:

8. **Was it worth playing:** would you play another session, and what decides it. The best moment and the worst. What
   felt like a chore. Reasons, not a score.

**Your journal,** as well as the guide. Then you're a regular, and the journal is your own notes from earlier sessions.
Nobody says whether the game is the same as last time. Don't hunt for differences: play, and say what you met. Add to
your report:

9. **Against last time:** what went differently from what your journal led you to expect: quicker, slower, easier,
   harder, new, missing. Say what you saw, never why it might be.
10. **Your notes, rewritten:** the whole journal written again as you'd want to read it before your next session, at
    most twenty lines. It replaces the old one, so drop what no longer holds.
